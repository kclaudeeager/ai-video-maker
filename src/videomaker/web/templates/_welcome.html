One project, two cuts, three gates. You give it a topic; it writes a script, speaks it, finds a shot for every line, and cuts that into a full-length video and a vertical Short of under three minutes — from the same script and the same footage, reframed rather than squeezed.

How a project runs

{# The diagram. A list, not a picture: it is the same rail the dashboard draws, it reads correctly with no CSS at all, and a screen reader gets the sequence rather than "image". #}
  1. Your topic
  2. Scriptnarration, scene by scene
  3. Gate 1You read it
  4. Voice & shotsspoken takes, word timings, a clip per scene
  5. Gate 2You pick the shots
  6. Captions & assemblysubtitles, one timeline per aspect
  7. Gate 3You watch the preview
  8. Two files on your diskfinal_wide.mp4 and final_vertical.mp4

It stops at each gate and waits. Nothing is voiced, rendered or published without you — the gates are the product, not a safety rail bolted to it.
